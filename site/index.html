01 Philosophy

Design for the decision, not the screen.

Thirteen years across IBM, BT Group, Siemens and John Deere taught me one thing repeatedly: enterprise software rarely fails on aesthetics. It fails because the person who has to act can't find the thing they need to act on.

01

Start from intent, work backwards to interface

Before any wireframe I map what the user is actually trying to settle — the alert they must triage, the dispute they must close, the shift they must hand over. Layout follows from that. It's why supervisors scan Where → Who before Severity, and why the designs I ship put location first.

02

Compress the cycle, never the evidence

I use AI to do the slow parts faster — coding interview transcripts, drafting documentation, generating prototype variants — so the team spends its hours on judgement instead of transcription. 147 field observations, coded and affinity-grouped in a fraction of the usual time, with the audit trail left visible for whoever challenges the number.

03

Governance is a set of agreements, not a gate

A design system only holds if the people shipping against it agreed to it. I run 3-in-a-box — PM, engineering and design deciding together — and write the rules down at token level so drift is a diff, not an argument.

Less rework. Higher velocity. Impact you can put a number on.
